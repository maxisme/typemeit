about

free push-to-talk dictation for the mac

less is more. type me it does one thing: you hold the fn key, speak, let go, and what you said is typed where your cursor is. it runs on your mac, costs nothing, and has no account to make.

why

there are a lot of dictation apps for the mac. most of them come with an account, a subscription, a choice of models, prompts to write and a server somewhere that hears your voice. each of those is a thing to set up, a thing to pay for, or a thing to trust.

i wanted one that types what i said. so type me it has a key, a speech model and a history, and stops there. the list of every dictation app for the mac shows where that puts it.

how it works

hold fn and the app records from the microphone. let go and the recording is transcribed on your mac by nvidia's parakeet model, running inside the app through transcribe.cpp. the text is pasted into whichever app has the cursor. the audio is discarded.

if you turn on apple intelligence in settings, apple's on-device model tidies the transcript before it is pasted. that model runs on your mac too.

after a paste, the app watches that text field for a moment. if you fix a word, it learns the correction and gets it right next time. that is the only training it does, and every learned word can be undone.

the history tab keeps the text of past dictations, as many as you choose, including none. the insights tab counts them.

what it costs

nothing. no trial, no tier, no sign-in. updates arrive through the app from its github releases, and you can turn the check off.

what it does not do

  • show text while you are still speaking. it types when you let go.
  • send anything to a server. there is no cloud model to switch to.
  • take prompts, modes or custom instructions.
  • transcribe files or record meetings.
  • run on windows, linux or ios. it needs macos 26 on apple silicon.
  • understand voice commands like "delete that".

parakeet covers 25 european languages. if yours is not one of them, one of the whisper-based apps on the list will serve you better.

where the code is

the source is public at github.com/maxisme/typemeit. the learning and insights code is a port of handy's, which is the free, open-source dictation app that got this part right first. the rest is swift, written for the mac.

the privacy page lists every file the app writes and both servers it talks to.
